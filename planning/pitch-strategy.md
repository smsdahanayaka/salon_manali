# Pitch Strategy

This project is **speculative pitch work**, not a commissioned job — see
[[salon-manali]] memory §0 for the full context. We build a compelling
website first, then present it to the Devinuwara branch's owner; it only
becomes a real engagement if they say yes. This file captures how that
changes the plan: what to lead with, what content to use in the meantime,
and what "done" means for this phase.

Last updated: 2026-08-17

---

## 1. What "done" means for this phase

Not a live public launch. The deliverable is **a demo good enough that the
owner says yes** — a working, professional-feeling website (real domain not
required yet, could be a preview link/local build) that:

1. Looks and feels like a real, credible business site, not a mockup
2. Uses honest content — real verified facts where we have them, clearly
   non-deceptive placeholders where we don't (see §3)
3. Makes the business case obvious to the owner within the pitch itself,
   not just through the visual design

## 2. The sales case — what to lead with

Pulled directly from [[competitor-research]] and [[industry-findings]].
These are the arguments that should shape both the demo's content and
however it gets presented to the owner:

- **"You're invisible next to salons with your own name."** The Kurunegala
  (98.6K FB likes) and Nittambuwa (71.6K) "Salon Manali" branches dominate
  any search for the business name — and even *this branch's own* Facebook
  page is tagged generically as "Matara," not "Devinuwara." A real site is
  the only way to reliably show up as *this specific salon* in local
  search. This is a problem the owner almost certainly feels (customers
  confused, calls meant for another branch) but may not have language for —
  naming it clearly is likely the single strongest hook.
- **"None of your direct local competitors have a real website."** Salon
  Mouni (same town), Salon Roo, Salon Sanjaya, Salon Shiny — all
  Facebook/directory-only. Only Eueni has a real site, and even it hides
  pricing and has no visible gallery on its listing. A well-built site
  makes this branch look more established than everyone nearby except
  Eueni, essentially overnight.
- **"You could show pricing when nobody else in the area does."** No
  Southern Province bridal salon researched shows even "starting from"
  bridal pricing. This is a free, low-effort differentiator once real
  numbers are available.
- **"Your customers are already booking by WhatsApp — the site should just
  make that one tap away."** Low-effort, high-credibility addition; matches
  confirmed regional booking norms ([[industry-findings]] §5).
- **Concrete, low-cost next steps she can say yes to** — a Google Business
  Profile claim for the correct address, WhatsApp click-to-chat, a starting
  price tier. Framing the pitch as "here's what's easy to fix first" lowers
  the barrier to a yes versus presenting it as an all-or-nothing purchase.

## 3. Content strategy for the demo (placeholder vs. real)

We do not have owner access to real photos, exact pricing, or hours. The
rule: **be genuinely useful and credible without fabricating specific
claims.**

| Content | Approach for the demo |
|---|---|
| Address, phone, category, bridal focus | **Use as-is** — these are independently verified public facts (Tanagalle Road, Devinuwara, Matara; 071 285 8857) |
| Bridal photos | Use tasteful, clearly-appropriate stock/placeholder bridal photography in the house style, OR (preferred once feasible) publicly-viewable photos already on the branch's own Facebook page, credited as theirs — never present stock photos as if they were this salon's actual work once the site goes live/public. For the pitch demo shown privately to the owner, clearly-labeled placeholders are fine and expected — the point is to show the *structure and quality bar*, with an explicit note that real photos drop in once they say yes. |
| Pricing | Use researched market-typical ranges framed as illustrative ("e.g., bridal packages from LKR X" sourced from comparable regional salons), clearly marked as a placeholder to replace with their real numbers — do not present invented numbers as their actual prices |
| Hours | Use a reasonable placeholder (e.g. "9:00 AM – 5:30 PM" per the pattern seen at other branches) marked TBC |
| Testimonials/reviews | **Do not fabricate.** Leave the section structurally present but empty/"coming soon," or use a generic industry stat/trust element instead (e.g. years-in-market framing) rather than invent a quote from a named person |
| "Brides styled" counts, awards, etc. | **Do not invent.** Leave out entirely until the owner can confirm real numbers |

## 4. Presentation plan (draft — refine closer to pitch time)

- Prepare the demo as a shareable link or an in-person/video walkthrough —
  decide format once the build is far enough along
- Lead with the naming/visibility problem (§2) before showing the site
  itself — the pitch is stronger if the owner *feels the problem* first
- Have the "what changes once you say yes" list ready (real photos, real
  pricing, real hours, WhatsApp number confirmed, Google Business Profile
  claim) so it's clear this demo is a draft, not a bait-and-switch
- No pricing/commercial terms for the actual sale are decided yet — flag
  this as a decision the user will need to make separately before the
  actual pitch conversation

## 5. What NOT to do

- Don't publish the demo site publicly under the salon's name before the
  owner has agreed — it should stay a private preview until there's a deal
- Don't claim Google Business Profile, social accounts, or any real-world
  presence on the salon's behalf without their authorization — research
  and recommend these, don't unilaterally create them
- Don't fabricate specific factual claims (see §3) even for a "just a
  demo" — a demo with fake testimonials or invented review counts is a bad
  look if the owner (or their customers) ever compares it against reality
